# Rate My Bites Story Bible

## Creative mission

Food is the setting. People, relationships, humor, memories, and emotion are the heart of every episode. A completed episode should feel less like finishing a quiz and more like sharing a meal with people worth remembering.

## Emotional ingredients

Each episode should create:

- interest in the people before the answer
- humor rooted in personality
- warmth between characters
- one relevant thing to learn or notice
- a fair discovery built from the conversation
- a satisfying celebration when the mystery is solved

Not every scene needs every ingredient. The complete episode should have all six.

## Writing tone

Write warm, human, conversational, specific, sincere, and broadly appropriate dialogue. Prefer a small recognizable detail over generic corporate copy. Keep lines concise enough to work at cinematic pacing.

## Characters

- Give every person a recognizable voice and point of view.
- Let dialogue reveal personality, not merely deliver clues.
- Connect memories to food naturally: who made it, where it was shared, or why it matters now.
- Give characters wants that can overlap, conflict, or change.
- Never reduce a character to an answer key.

The current episodes use three diners so they fit the established prediction, scoring, reveal, animation, and responsive systems.

## Pup, the Bite Buddy

Confirmed current behavior:

- Pup is the Game Master and warm detective guide.
- Pup frames the mission, notices shifts in the room, and encourages the player.
- Pup does not give away the correct answer.
- Pup can be lightly playful, but the diners remain the emotional center.
- Spoken narration is optional; all necessary information remains available as text.
- Pup’s voice and developer tools keep their existing browser and production safeguards.

Keep Pup observant, concise, encouraging, and slightly theatrical. Do not invent a larger canon unless a future approved story needs it.

## Humor

- Be affectionate rather than cruel.
- Build jokes from personality, habits, timing, and relationships.
- Keep humor brief enough not to interrupt gameplay.
- Do not ridicule protected traits, disability, illness, appearance, culture, or identity.

## Emotion

- Earn emotion through relationships, traditions, memories, or personal meaning.
- Keep it natural rather than manipulative.
- Avoid excessive tragedy.
- Let an emotional beat support the mystery; do not make it swallow the episode.

## Food observations

- Keep facts and observations relevant and concise.
- Frame individual experiences carefully.
- Verify factual claims before shipping.
- Do not make unsupported medical or cultural claims.
- Prefer a useful observation inside a character’s experience over a detached trivia lecture.

## Avoid

- copyright infringement or implied official third-party affiliation
- stereotypes or insensitive humor
- excessive exposition and filler dialogue
- clues that directly announce an answer
- emotionally empty stories that are only technically complete
- invented private details about real people

## Future AI-assisted workflow

1. A human supplies approved story ingredients.
2. AI may produce a draft.
3. A human reviews facts, tone, rights, clues, and emotional quality.
4. A human edits and approves the final episode.
5. Only approved static content ships.

Sprint 3 does not generate any episode content at runtime.
